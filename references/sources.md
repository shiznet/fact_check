# Source handling guidance

## Web pages
- Search to discover candidates, then open relevant sources before relying on their contents.
- Inspect publisher, date, context, links, and the relevant passage.
- Find the original source cited by an article when feasible.
- Watch for SEO pages, copied/scraped text, outdated pages, and unattributed claims.
- Ranking or index presence does not establish reliability. Label snippet-only access as a limitation.
- Record redirects, inaccessible pages, and paywalls where material.

## Social media
Applies to Xiaohongshu/RED, short-video platforms, forums, microblogs, comments, and reposts.
- Treat posts as claims or leads, not automatically as evidence of the underlying proposition.
- Preserve account/post URL, date, and exact text/image excerpt when available.
- Separate first-person experience from causal explanation and population-level claims.
- Check for advertising, affiliate promotion, sponsorship, satire, or reposting when evidence supports it.
- Inspect image, caption, and comments separately. Comments may provide leads but are not automatic corroboration.
- Trace whether multiple posts derive from one original source. Do not infer motive or account authenticity without adequate evidence.
- If the original post is unavailable, state whether the review is limited to a screenshot, transcription, or secondary copy.

## Documents and research
- Identify title, author/publisher, date, version, and document type where available.
- Read relevant sections in context, including footnotes, tables, appendices, definitions, and methodology.
- For research, distinguish abstract claims from methods, results, limitations, corrections, and retractions.
- For charts/tables, verify labels, units, denominators, time range, and whether values are estimated or observed.
- If OCR or extraction is incomplete, inspect page images when available and disclose uncertainty.
- A document's own assertion is not independent confirmation of that assertion.

## Code repositories and technical sources
- Identify repository, branch/tag/commit, release, license, and environment when material.
- Prefer source code, tests, official documentation, release notes, and reproducible behavior over unsupported summaries.
- Check whether a claim applies to the current branch, a released version, or only an unreleased change.
- Issues and comments show reports or discussions, not necessarily confirmed behavior.
- Distinguish intended features from merged changes, released features, and features enabled in production.
- Do not infer a whole system's behavior from a small excerpt without noting missing context.
