# Domain guidance

Use general claim/evidence rules by default. Add the relevant specialist checks below when applicable.

## General
- Check the original data or methodology where feasible.
- Separate anecdotes from generalizations.
- For causal claims, distinguish causation from correlation and confounding.
- Compare like with like: definitions, denominators, periods, populations, and conditions.
- For claims of consensus, establish the relevant expert/community group and evidence of consensus.

## Medical and health
- Prefer public-health agencies, drug labels, clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and well-designed primary studies.
- Check population, dose, duration, comparator, endpoints, adverse events, and study limitations.
- Distinguish hazard, association, causal effect, absolute risk, and relative risk.
- Do not infer an individual diagnosis or treatment suitability from general studies.
- Treat testimonials as reports of experience, not proof of efficacy or causation.
- Check jurisdiction and currentness. For urgent symptoms, prioritize appropriate urgent care over extended fact-checking.

## Legal and policy
- Identify jurisdiction, relevant date, affected entity/person, and exact rule or procedure.
- Prefer current primary legal texts, official government guidance, court decisions, regulator publications, and official policy documents.
- Distinguish enacted law from proposals, agency guidance, commentary, and common practice.
- Check commencement dates, transitional provisions, exceptions, and amendments.
- Do not generalize rules across jurisdictions or present general information as individualized legal advice.

## Finance and economics
- Identify asset/product, currency, period, risk profile, and relevant jurisdiction.
- Prefer regulator filings, audited statements, official datasets, prospectuses, and transparent methodology.
- Distinguish historical performance from future returns; nominal from real values; absolute from relative risk.
- Check fees, liquidity, leverage, conflicts of interest, and assumptions. Do not treat forecasts as guarantees.

## Technology and security
- Identify product/edition/version, date, runtime, operating system, configuration, and deployment context.
- Prefer official documentation, release notes, standards, issue trackers, source code, advisories, and reproducible tests.
- Distinguish documented behavior, observed behavior, inferred implementation, merged code, released features, and production deployment.
- For security claims, check affected versions, threat model, advisories, and exploitability. Absence of known vulnerabilities does not prove safety.

## News and current events
- Confirm publication time, event time, location, and whether information remains current.
- Prefer primary statements, records, filings, direct footage with provenance, and multiple independent reputable reports.
- Separate confirmed facts from allegations, anonymous-source reporting, analysis, and speculation.
- Check corrections and updates. Treat early reports as provisional when events are developing.
